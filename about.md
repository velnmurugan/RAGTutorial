# About

I'm a Wissenschaftlicher Mitarbeiter (research associate) at the
**Institut für Erziehungswissenschaft, Universität Stuttgart**, in the
department of Berufspädagogik mit Schwerpunkt Technikdidaktik (Technical
& Vocational Education, Technology Didactics). My research sits at the
intersection of AI and technical/vocational education — developing and
evaluating trustworthy AI-supported learning systems that aim to be
meaningful, transparent, and reliable, rather than just convincing.

## Background

I came to this from computer vision and robotics, not education. During
my M.Sc. in Mechatronics at Universität Siegen (2021–2025) — with a
focus on robotics, deep learning, computer vision, control engineering,
sensor technology, and embedded systems — I completed my thesis in
collaboration with **Fraunhofer IPA**, contributing to the development
and evaluation of computer vision models for object detection and pose
estimation.

That work grew out of an earlier project at Fraunhofer IPA (Wissenschaftliche
Hilfskraft, 2025), where I designed a system for analyzing hand
movements in industrial assembly tasks — using pose estimation and 3D
mesh models to give real-time feedback on manual assembly technique.

Before mechatronics, I trained as a mechanical engineer (B.E., Anna
University, India, 2016–2020), which is where the dynamic modeling and
simulation background comes from — and still shapes how I think about
grounding AI systems in something physically or empirically real, rather
than treating them as black boxes.

## Now

I'm interested in what happens when those two threads — physically
grounded AI, and how people actually learn technical skills — meet. That
question is what *Grounded AI*, the RAG tutorial series on this site, is
partly an exploration of: how retrieval-augmented systems can scaffold
learning instead of just handing over answers.
